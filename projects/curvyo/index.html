Project case study / Curvyo

Curvyo: developing vector tools with AI agents

A vector tool in early development. My focus is product direction, technical decisions, specifications, reviews and testing of agent-developed software.

The idea

Turning a vector drawing into a physical object often requires several applications. Curvyo aims to connect design and manufacturing: eventually for laser cutters, plotters, embroidery machines and CNC mills. The first planned release focuses on a local laser workflow with GRBL G-code.

My role

Curvyo is developed entirely by AI agents. I act as customer and technical lead: setting direction, making technical decisions, writing specifications and checking results through reviews, testing and bug reports. Responsibility for those decisions stays with me.

The architecture decision

The document model, geometry and UI logic live in Rust crates. Tauri hosts the desktop application, while WebAssembly connects the browser interface. Development is specification-driven and supported by CI checks. The approach is local-first and offline-capable: desktop comes first, with browser and mobile companions lower in priority.

What works today

The public README lists creating, opening and saving local projects, Bézier paths, geometric primitives, selection and transformations. Drawing and editing are already possible. The repository and specification index document the detailed feature status.

What is still planned

Curvyo is not yet a complete manufacturing workflow. The documented status lists SVG import and export, machine profiles, job preview and G-code output as missing. Plotters, embroidery and CNC are later goals. Distinguishing the product idea from current behaviour is part of clear technical communication.

Why I show this project

It makes my work on technical decisions and verifiable specifications visible. It is my own development approach, rather than evidence of a general productivity increase from AI. The managers workshop similarly examines a technology through its benefits, constraints and adoption effort.
